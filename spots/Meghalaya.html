Meghalaya

The Cloud-Wrapped Kingdom of Canyons & Subterranean Rivers

About the Abode of Clouds

Perched across the high, wet plateaus of Northeast India, the regions of Cherrapunji (Sohra) and Mawsynram are globally famous for being the wettest places on Earth. Carved out by centuries of massive rain totals, this limestone landscape is an extreme wilderness. For adventure trackers, it serves as India's ultimate arena for technical deep-cave navigation, steep canyon trekking, and river-canyon exploration.

The Vibe

Meghalaya carries an incredibly atmospheric, prehistoric energy. Thick walls of mountain mist roll constantly across massive green gorges, dropping down into deep, shadow-filled valleys. It is a playground designed for raw exploration—where damp, grueling jungle tracks lead to massive underground cave networks and centuries-old living root bridges woven over raging forest torrents.

Epic Adventures to Try

Technical Limestone Caving

Strap on tactical helmets and headlamps to penetrate deep, unlit cave systems like Krem Mawmluh. Squeeze through tight limestone slots, wade through deep underground pools, and map ancient stalactite chambers.

Nongriat Living Root Trek

Descend over 3,500 grueling, steep concrete steps into the heart of a sheer forest canyon. Cross suspension bridges to reach the legendary Double-Decker Living Root Bridge woven by Khasi tribes.

Extreme Canyoneering

Navigate the deep river beds of Cherrapunji. Rappel down wet rock faces, leap off high stone shelves into pristine turquoise pools, and slide down natural rock chutes carved by heavy water currents.

David Scott Wilderness Trail

Trek along an ancient 16-kilometer horse trail dating back to the British era. Cross spectacular stone arch bridges, clear mountain streams, and vast, open pine-forested plateaus.

Useful Information

  • Best Time for Action: October to April when the dry season opens up deep cave routes and lowers river hazards; completely avoid monsoons due to flooding hazards.
  • Caving Safety Hazards: Never enter any wild cave systems without certified local Khasi guides and proper headlamps. Sudden weather shifts can cause subterranean spaces to fill with water rapidly.
  • Grip & Footwear Selection: The jungle stairs and limestone beds are heavily coated in damp moss. Use tactical footwear with high-traction rubber treads to prevent slips.
  • Physical Conditioning: Deep valley routes (like Nongriat) require incredible leg endurance to climb back up. Ensure you are prepared for steep, continuous vertical step ascents.
