Manali

The Gateway to High-Altitude Playground

About Manali

Nestled in the Beas River Valley at an altitude of 2,050 meters, Manali is India’s premier destination for backpackers, trekkers, and adrenaline seekers. Flanked by towering, snow-capped Himalayan peaks and dense pine forests, it serves as the ultimate launching pad for deep wilderness exploration into Lahaul, Spiti, and Ladakh.

The Vibe

Manali strikes a beautiful balance between raw, rugged wilderness and laid-back alpine culture. In Old Manali, the scent of fresh eucalyptus mixes with wood-fire cafes playing acoustic rock. Down by the riverbeds, it is all about grit and gear—harnesses drying in the sun, off-road jeeps prepping for mountain passes, and travelers exchanging stories of thin air and wild trails.

Epic Adventures to Try

Paragliding in Solang

Launch off giant alpine ridges and float high above the Solang Valley. Experience breathtaking bird's-eye views of the snow-dusted Dhauladhar range.

Battle freezing white-water rapids rushing down from the glaciers. The stretch from Pirdi down to Jhiri offers thrilling Grade II and III rapids.

Hampta Pass Trekking

A grueling but magical transition trek that takes you from the lush green meadows of Manali right into the stark, barren desert landscapes of Spiti Valley.

Solang Quad Biking

Tear through dirt trails, rocky riverbeds, and muddy mountain paths on high-powered ATVs designed to dominate rough mountain terrain.

Useful Information

  • Best Time for Action: March to June for paragliding and trekking; December to February for snowboarding and winter sports.
  • Atmospheric Pressure: Altitude sickness is rare here, but stay hydrated if you plan to ascend immediately to Atal Tunnel or Rohtang Pass (3,978m).
  • Local Transit: Rent a local 4x4 or a mountain bike to navigate the steep, rocky inclines of Old Manali seamlessly.
  • Permits: Rohtang Pass requires a special road permit. Ensure you book a day in advance if planning a high-altitude drive.
